Hybrid quantum--classical physics-informed neural networks (QCPINNs) are usually
reported as efficient because they use fewer trainable parameters than a classical PINN.
We ask what this saving costs in wall-clock time.
On a one-dimensional seawater temperature diffusion benchmark, we compare a classical PINN
with four hybrid configurations that differ in qubit count and circuit depth.
\emph{Parameter count:} the quantum branch adds only 16--64 trainable parameters, and the
hybrids have 2421--2717 parameters in total against 4353 for the classical baseline
(a 38--44\% reduction), almost all of which comes from the smaller classical part.
