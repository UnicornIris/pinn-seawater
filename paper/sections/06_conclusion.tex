We set out to test whether a hybrid quantum-classical PINN built on a
previously proposed circuit ansatz matches classical PINN performance on a
benchmark diffusion PDE once wall-clock training time is accounted for
alongside accuracy (RQ1), and, if not, whether the gap traces to training
hyperparameters or to the quantum circuit's structure (RQ2). It does not
match classical performance: across all six boundary-condition and
forward/inverse scenarios, the quantum branch is both less accurate and
53--89$\times$ slower in wall-clock time, and this gap survives targeted
hyperparameter tuning of initialization scale and learning-rate schedule.
A subsequent sweep of qubit count and circuit depth, instrumented with a
direct measurement of the circuit-only gradient norm, shows that circuit
structure does matter, but not in a way that cleanly fits either a pure
expressivity-limited or a pure barren-plateau account -- the best-accuracy
configuration in our sweep also has the smallest measured gradient norm,
a pattern more consistent with the small-system-size end of the
expressibility-gradient relationship of \citet{holmes2022expressibility}
than with either account taken alone. Beyond this specific PDE, the
methodological point we think most likely to generalize is the one
developed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}: efficiency claims for hybrid
quantum-classical PINNs in the current literature are reported almost
exclusively via parameter count or training epochs, and wall-clock time is
absent or incomplete in every closely related paper we examined. Our own
results show that accounting for it changes the practical picture, and we
suggest it become standard practice for evaluating this class of model.
